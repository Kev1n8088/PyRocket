PyRocket cooling channel geometry: parameter definitions
=========================================================

How the config parameters define the cooling channels, so a design can be rebuilt in CAD or another tool.
All lengths in metres in the config. Angles in degrees.


1. Reference surface and coordinates
------------------------------------
x          Axial coordinate along the engine axis. x = 0 is the injector face, increasing towards the nozzle exit.
r_gas(x)   Radius of the hot-gas surface (the chamber contour). It comes from the chamber inputs (D_c, D_t, D_e, L_cyl, r_2, r_1,
           r_n, phi_conv, phi_div, phi_e, nozzle_type, L_e).
Meridian   The contour line in the x-r plane, i.e. the line where a plane containing the engine axis cuts the wall. On the
           cylindrical section it is parallel to the axis. On the cones it is inclined by the local wall angle beta.
Stations   The contour is sampled every step_size (finer near the throat). Every parameter that is a function of x is evaluated
           at these stations.

Channels run the full contour length, from the injector face to the nozzle exit.


2. Radial build-up (all offsets are RADIAL, not normal to the wall)
------------------------------------------------------------------
t_w_i      Hot wall thickness: from the hot-gas surface to the channel bottom.      r_i = r_gas + t_w_i
h_c        Channel height: from the channel bottom to the channel top.              r_o = r_i + h_c
t_w_o      Outer wall (closeout) thickness: from the channel top to the outside.    r_shell = r_o + t_w_o

The offsets are taken in the radial direction (perpendicular to the axis), not perpendicular to the wall. On a cone with wall
angle beta, the true thickness measured normal to the wall is t_w_i * cos(beta). For example, 0.8 mm radial on a 30 degree cone
is 0.69 mm normal. To reproduce the model exactly, offset radially in CAD. For a wall that is t_w_i thick normal to the surface
instead, divide by cos(beta).

h_c, t_w_i and psi may be constants or functions of x. t_w_o is a constant.


3. Channel count and circumferential split
------------------------------------------
n          Total number of channels around the circumference. For two-pass cooling this counts both passes (n/2 forward,
           n/2 return) and must be even.
psi        Fill factor: the fraction of the circumference taken up by channels. Measure it in a cut PERPENDICULAR TO THE ENGINE
           AXIS (x = const), at the channel bottom radius r_i. The remaining 1 - psi is ribs (lands).

           Angular pitch of one channel plus one rib:   2*pi / n
           Angle of one channel:                        2*pi * psi / n
           Angle of one rib:                            2*pi * (1 - psi) / n

Channel and rib side walls are RADIAL: they lie on lines through the engine axis. Each channel is therefore an annular sector,
slightly wider at the top (r_o) than at the bottom (r_i), and the same holds for the ribs.

With a constant psi, channel and rib widths scale with the radius: narrowest at the throat, widest in the chamber.

channel_width   Optional alternative to psi: the channel width at the channel bottom, measured NORMAL TO THE FLOW (see
                section 4). It can be a constant or a function of x. When set, it overrides psi, and psi follows at each
                station as
                    psi = channel_width * n / (2*pi * r_i * cos(alpha))
                A constant channel_width gives constant-width channels with ribs that grow with the radius. Leave it as None
                to use psi.


4. Helix
--------
helix_angle     Angle alpha between the channel and the local meridian (not the engine axis; on the cones these differ).
                0 means straight axial channels. It can be a constant or a function of x.
                    The angle is defined at the channel centreline radius  R_c = r_i + h_c/2.
                    A constant alpha is a loxodrome (rhumb line) on the surface of revolution: it crosses every meridian at
                    the same angle. The wrap angle around the axis grows as
                        d(theta)/ds = tan(alpha) / R_c        (s = arc length along the meridian)
                    so the channel wraps fastest where the radius is smallest (the throat).
                    The model does not use handedness (left or right hand). All channels share the same helix and run
                    parallel.

helix_pitch_deg_per_mm
                Alternative to helix_angle: a constant twist rate in degrees of rotation per mm of AXIAL distance. It
                overrides helix_angle when set. The local angle then follows as
                    tan(alpha) = R_c * pitch[rad/m] * cos(beta)
                so alpha varies along the chamber. Set it to None to use helix_angle.

Widths along the flow vs. across the flow:
    The circumferential widths from section 3 are measured in a cut perpendicular to the axis. A channel crossing that cut at
    angle alpha is narrower across its flow direction by cos(alpha):

        Channel width normal to flow, bottom:   w_c = 2*pi * r_i * psi       * cos(alpha) / n
        Channel width normal to flow, top:      2*pi * r_o * psi       * cos(alpha) / n
        Rib width normal to flow, bottom:       w_r = 2*pi * r_i * (1 - psi) * cos(alpha) / n
        Rib width normal to flow, top:          2*pi * r_o * (1 - psi) * cos(alpha) / n

    Use the normal-to-flow values for the manufacturing limits (minimum channel and rib width). The optimiser reports these
    values, and "channel_width" means this one (bottom, normal to flow).

Channel path length = meridional length / cos(alpha)  (per station, integrated along the contour).


5. Flow cross-section (derived, for checking a CAD model)
--------------------------------------------------------
All values are normal to the flow, per channel:
    Flow area:              A = psi * pi * (r_o^2 - r_i^2) * cos(alpha) / n
    Wetted perimeter:       U = (bottom width) + (top width) + 2*h_c
    Hydraulic diameter:     D_h = 4*A / U

The model uses sharp corners: no fillets or draft. Wall roughness comes from the material in MaterialLib.py.


6. Two-pass cooling
-------------------
two_pass = True   Channels alternate around the circumference: forward, return, forward, return, and so on.
                  n/2 channels carry each pass. Both passes have the same cross-section and helix.
start_idx         The end where coolant enters the forward channels and leaves the return channels: 0 = injector end,
                  -1 = nozzle exit end. The turnaround manifold is at the opposite end. For single-pass cooling it is
                  simply the inlet end.
turnaround_loss   Pressure loss of the turnaround manifold, as a multiple of the channel dynamic pressure 0.5*rho*v^2
                  (default 1.5). The model does not include the manifold geometry itself.


7. Minimum set of numbers to hand to someone rebuilding a design
---------------------------------------------------------------
    - Chamber contour r_gas(x). Either the contour inputs listed in section 1, or an x, r point list.
    - n, and single- or two-pass (with the inlet end).
    - h_c, t_w_i, t_w_o, each stated as RADIAL offsets.
    - psi (constant or as a function of x), or channel_width normal to the flow at the channel bottom.
    - helix_angle measured from the meridian at R_c = r_i + h_c/2, or helix_pitch_deg_per_mm.
    - Radial side walls and sharp corners, unless you deliberately deviate from the model.

Give psi and h_c to full precision rather than rounded values. The 2D solver meshes the exact geometry, and a rounding in the
6th digit changes the mesh, which moves results by a fraction of a kelvin.
